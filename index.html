Job market paper

Gender Visibility, Credit, and the Choice to Collaborate

with Alexander Coutts

Collaboration pools contributions, meaning biases can influence credit. Ability stereotypes may penalize women’s joint work, but beliefs about who collaborates can also shift attribution, even in reverse. In experiments varying gender visibility and selection into collaboration, ability beliefs show no gender gap, yet employers who see gender rank male above female teammates only 39 percent of the time. The penalty persists under random team assignment, follows the mistaken belief that women collaborate more, and concentrates where contributions are ambiguous. High-performing men, not women, collaborate less, though both expect employers to favor men. Fixing one bias does not prevent the next.

Work in progress

Algorithmic Mediation and Fair Allocation Models

with Onur Kesten and Selçuk Özyurt

Abstract

Mediation is widely used as an alternative to courts, yet legal theorists argue that power imbalances and biases can disadvantage less powerful parties. We run an experiment comparing an algorithmic mediation mechanism with unstructured bargaining, and vary whether disputants can see each other’s gender. We test the mechanism’s effects on truthful reporting, fairness, and welfare.

Measuring the Impacts of Conflict Resolution Skills on Academic Success

with Selçuk Özyurt and Fulya Türk

Abstract

We conduct a randomized field experiment with Türkiye’s Ministry of National Education in 15 public schools in Istanbul, with more than 1,000 students. The study evaluates how training in conflict resolution skills affects adolescents’ academic achievement and socio-emotional skills.

The Design and Pricing of Customized Data

Abstract

Data is increasingly traded as a good, yet its value depends on buyers’ specific informational needs. We study how a monopolist should design and price information when buyers face the same uncertainty but care about different preferred states. We show that a shared experiment can maximize value and derive optimal pricing under monopoly, first-degree, and second-degree price discrimination. Fully revealing experiments are sometimes optimal, while in other cases deliberately distorted signals increase revenue. The results provide guidance for how credit reporting and analytics platforms should version and price data.

Experiments

I design and program my own experiments in Qualtrics with custom HTML, JavaScript and PHP. My online experiments have collected data from more than 5,000 participants.

Research experiments

Gender visibility and the choice to collaborate

Online experiment, 2,812 participants, preregistered (AEARCTR-0015798)

Candidates complete a reasoning task and choose to work alone or in a team; employers then rank and hire them. We vary whether employers hear candidates’ gender through a voice cue, and whether teams are chosen by candidates or assigned at random.

Algorithmic mediation versus free-form bargaining

Online experiment with real-time interaction, preregistered (AEARCTR-0017830)

Pairs resolve disputes either through an algorithmic mediation mechanism or through unstructured bargaining, with or without seeing each other’s gender. Built in Qualtrics with SMARTRIQS so participants interact live.
