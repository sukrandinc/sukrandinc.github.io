Who Gets Credit? Bias and Attribution in Team vs. Solo Work

(with Alexander Coutts)

Collaboration boosts productivity but makes it harder to recognize individual contributions especially when biases, like gender bias, distort evaluations. We run an experiment where individuals choose to work alone or in teams, knowing their performance will be judged by a third-party evaluator. By comparing anonymous settings to ones where gender is revealed, we show how visibility affects collaboration decisions. Our findings highlight the impact of gender bias on evaluation and how people respond to it, offering insights for building fairer and more effective workplaces.
