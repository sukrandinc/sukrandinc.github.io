{% endif %} {% include base_path %} ## Working Papers **Who Gets Credit? Bias and Attribution in Team vs. Solo Work** (with Alexander Coutts) Collaboration is a key driver of productivity in organizations and intellectual work, yet it can make it harder to recognize individual contributions. Bias—especially gender bias—can further distort evaluations, shaping both how people choose to collaborate and how their work is assessed. In this study, we run an experiment to understand how individuals decide whether to work alone or in teams, knowing their past performance will be judged by a third-party evaluator. Evaluators, in turn, must weigh the trade-off between the potential benefits of selecting a collaborative worker and the uncertainty about their individual abilities. To explore the role of gender in these decisions, we compare a baseline anonymous setting to a treatment where gender is publicly revealed, creating common knowledge of potential biases. Our findings reveal how men and women adjust their willingness to collaborate when they know their gender is visible, shedding light on both the reality of gender biases in evaluation and how accurately individuals perceive them. Ultimately, our results highlight how collaboration can be made more effective—both overall and in ways that account for gender dynamics—helping organizations design fairer and more productive work environments. --- ## Work in Progress **Measuring the Impacts of Interpersonal Conflict Management and Dispute Resolution Skills Training on Academic Success: A Field Experiment** (with Fulya Türk and Selcuk Ozyurt) **Can Online Dispute Resolution Advance Gender Equality?
