{% endif %} {% include base_path %} ## 📝 Working Papers ### **Who Gets Credit? Bias and Attribution in Team vs. Solo Work** with Alexander Coutts Collaboration is a key driver of productivity in organizations and intellectual work. However, it often obscures individual contributions, making evaluations challenging. Gender bias can further distort assessments, affecting both collaborative choices and how work is perceived. In this experimental study, we examine how individuals choose between working alone or in teams, knowing their performance will later be judged by a third-party evaluator. Evaluators must balance the expected benefits of team-based work against the uncertainty of assessing individual performance within a team. To explore the role of gender, we compare a baseline anonymous setting to a treatment where gender is publicly revealed. This enables us to study both actual and perceived gender bias in evaluation. We find that men and women strategically adjust their collaboration preferences when gender is visible, revealing nuanced insights about fairness, incentives, and social perception. Our findings inform the design of more equitable work environments and evaluation systems that promote both fairness and productivity. --- ## 🔧 Work in Progress ### **Measuring the Impacts of Interpersonal Conflict Management and Dispute Resolution Skills Training on Academic Success: A Field Experiment** with Fulya Türk and Selcuk Ozyurt --- ### **Can Online Dispute Resolution Advance Gender Equality?
